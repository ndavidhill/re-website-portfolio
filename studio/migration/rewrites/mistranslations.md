### Mistranslations: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### How does communication behave with genuine care — when the system fails the people who need it most?

During COVID, some official Australian health messaging was run through Google Translate instead of accredited translators, producing nonsense for non-English-speaking communities and disempowering already vulnerable people. With 39% of Australians culturally and linguistically diverse — and more likely to miss critical health information — this wasn't a translation glitch. It was a behaviour problem: a communications system that excluded the people it was meant to protect.

#### The Designed Behaviour

### We designed a project that behaves as the inclusive model it argues for.

The Mistranslations Project was built to be the thing it advocates: a more inclusive way to communicate. Rather than lecture, it invited linguistically diverse creatives to reimagine COVID messaging in their own languages — engaging the people affected by the failures to demonstrate a better way to reach diverse communities.

#### Signature Behaviours

### How the project practised inclusion instead of preaching it.

### Hand the brief to the community

An identity, microsite and Instagram account shared the brief with diverse creatives; workshops drew submissions from TAFE students, and SBS coverage widened the call. The project behaves by giving voice, not just talking about it.

### Collaboration across difference

The brief paired creatives with non-creatives — including an English-speaking designer and a Djambarrpuyngu-speaking park ranger, whose insight that 1.5m distancing was unworkable in shared housing proved why one-size-fits-all messaging fails.

### A wordmark that mistranslates

The identity harnesses and distorts the universal language of symbolism: in motion the wordmark becomes unreadable as alphabets tick over and sticker icons pile up — embodying the overload and confusion of pandemic communication.

#### Signature Moment: The poster exhibition

### 48 submissions, 26 languages, on the wall.

Eighteen of forty-eight submissions across twenty-six languages were shown at Melbourne Design Week — every poster for sale, proceeds to Connection Arts Space, which supports creatives from asylum-seeker and migrant backgrounds. As one artist put it: "Last Friday was one of my best moments in life."

#### The Proof

### A model for communicating with care — made real, not just argued.

By engaging the communities failed by the system, the project turned a critique into a working demonstration of inclusive communication — surfacing real stories, raising funds for migrant creatives, and proving tailored messaging reaches people that "good enough" translation never will.
