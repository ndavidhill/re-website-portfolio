### Kit: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### How does a kids' money app behave so children actually want to use it — and grown-ups still trust it?

By 2022, cash wasn't king, crypto was confusing and the cost of living was its own conversation. Kit — a CommBank-backed money app for kids and their grown-ups — had to do something most financial brands can't: behave in a way a child genuinely wants to engage with, while earning the trust of the parent watching over their shoulder. Financial education only works if kids keep coming back.

#### The Designed Behaviour

### We designed the brand to behave like a kid in a grown-up's world.

We talked to 5–16 year-olds and learned kids are driven by what they want — and what they want is gloriously random. That insight became the brand idea: kids walking in a grown-up's world. The system behaves with exuberance and cheek, pulling children into Kit's universe while signalling enough credibility for the adults who pay for it.

#### Signature Behaviours

### Behaviours that win kids and reassure grown-ups at once.

### Kit, the money sidekick

A hero character drawn from the tittle of the "i" behaves as a cheerful, cheeky guide across the whole product — the smart money sidekick that makes financial learning feel like company, not a lesson.

### Built agile, in the product

With the app built in parallel, we co-created in Figma and Slack to shape the UI and journey directly — injecting personality at exactly the moments that matter, so the brand behaves as part of the experience rather than a skin on top.

### Playful and aspirational at once

A mixed-media language of illustration, photography and type — bright palette, a custom cut of Champ — lets kids step into a grown-up's world while nodding to a parent's nostalgia. Equal parts play and aspiration, so kids keep returning as they grow more independent.

#### The Proof

### A money brand kids choose — and grown-ups trust.

Kit launched in beta with a brand designed to behave for two audiences at once: playful enough to keep kids engaged, credible enough to earn grown-up trust. Its managing director put it simply — "I think you've nailed it. I think it's perfect."
