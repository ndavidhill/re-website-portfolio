# Re Case Study Rewrite Prompt

## Behaviour-Led Format: Truth → Behaviour → System → Proof

---

## INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE

You are rewriting brand case studies for **Re**, a brand and experience design studio.

Re's positioning is: **"Brand that behaves."**
Core belief: **Brands fundamentally drive behaviour. We design how they behave.**

Every case study must be rewritten using the following structure and rules. Do not deviate from this format.

---

## THE FORMAT

### [CLIENT NAME]: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### _[Unique subheading: a short, sharp question or provocation that names the specific behavioural tension for this client. e.g. "How does a telco behave like a premium experience when SIMs are sold at supermarkets?"]_

_1–2 paragraphs. Frame the business or brand problem as a BEHAVIOUR problem — not a visual or identity problem. Answer: "How does this brand need to behave differently to win?" Avoid generic strategy language. Make it feel specific, high-stakes, and commercially real._

#### The Designed Behaviour

### _[Unique subheading: name the core behavioural idea in a single declarative line. e.g. "We rebuilt 'Yes' as an engine — not a slogan."]_

_1–2 paragraphs. Describe the core behavioural idea Re designed. What was the central design decision? What did the brand need to DO — not just look like? Frame this as an intentional design of behaviour, not a visual refresh._

#### Signature Behaviours

### _[Unique subheading: frame what these behaviours collectively achieve. e.g. "Three rules for how the brand acts at every scale."]_

_A list of 3–5 specific, named behaviours Re designed into the system. Each should:_

- _Have a short, ownable name (e.g. "Behave with energy", "The Human Motion Curve")_
- _Describe what the brand was designed to DO at a specific touchpoint_
- _Feel like a design decision, not a brand value_

#### Signature Moment _(optional but preferred)_

### _[Unique subheading: name the moment specifically. e.g. "Retail reimagined: where the brand stops selling and starts connecting."]_

_Name and describe one specific, tangible moment where the behaviour is most powerfully felt. This could be a retail environment, a digital interaction, a motion system, a product feature, or a campaign. Make it concrete and specific._

#### The Proof

### _[Unique subheading: summarise the outcome as a behavioural statement. e.g. "The strongest brand in Australia — built to behave, not just appear."]_

_2–4 sentences. Outcomes, results, or impact. Can include metrics, awards, client quotes, or qualitative shifts. Frame as evidence that the behaviour worked — not just that the project was delivered._

---

## RULES FOR REWRITING

1. **Never lead with visual identity.** Always lead with the behavioural challenge or design decision.

2. **Replace "look and feel" language** with behavioural language:
   - ❌ "We refreshed the visual identity"
   - ✅ "We designed the brand to behave with more energy and intent"

3. **Name the behaviours specifically.** Don't say "we created a consistent brand." Say "we designed the brand to behave as a unified ecosystem across 14 markets."

4. **Use active, designed language.** The brand doesn't "feel" things — it "does" things.
   - ❌ "The brand feels more human"
   - ✅ "The brand moves and sounds with a rhythmic, call-and-response behaviour"

5. **Always connect to a commercial or cultural outcome.** Every behaviour should ladder up to a business result.

6. **Avoid startup language.** Re works with scale-ups and shake-ups. Language should feel board-level, not pitch-deck.

7. **Avoid generic brand agency language** such as: "holistic," "seamless," "authentic," "storytelling," "brand journey."

8. **The Signature Moment should be specific and tangible** — a real interaction, environment, or system that someone can picture or experience.

9. **Tone:** Collins-style. Confident, declarative, commercially sharp. Short sentences. No fluff.

---

## TONE REFERENCE

**Write like this:**

> "This wasn't a logo problem. It was a behaviour problem."
> "The brand doesn't just look human; it moves and sounds with a rhythmic, call-and-response behaviour."
> "We didn't just refresh an identity. We designed a unified behavioural system that made equality visible and felt."

**Not like this:**

> "We partnered with the client to develop a holistic brand refresh that authentically captured their story."
> "The new identity system creates a seamless experience across all touchpoints."

### OPTUS: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### How does a telco behave like a premium network experience — when SIMs are sold at supermarkets?

By 2020, streaming was second nature, 5G had arrived, and even supermarkets were selling SIMs. For Optus, the category was louder, faster, and more complex — so the brand had to do more than explain plans. It had to feel like connection: energetic, confident, human, and premium — at scale.

#### The Designed Behaviour

### We rebuilt "Yes" as an engine — not a slogan.

We centred the system on Optus' iconic "Yes" and rebuilt the brand to behave with more energy and intent across every touchpoint. The goal wasn't a new look — it was a clearer, more consistent experience of the brand in motion.

#### Signature Behaviours

### Three rules for how the brand acts at every scale.

- **Behave with energy:** "Yes" becomes the engine of a more energetic system — expressing dynamism and wit, not corporate telco polish.
- **Behave with confident clarity:** Colour simplified; typography used more boldly — so the brand communicates faster and cleaner in the moments that matter.
- **Behave at retail scale:** The system was designed to live in physical space, not just in comms — showing the true scale of Optus, not just what they sell.

#### Signature Moment: Retail Reimagined

### Where the brand stops selling and starts connecting.

In the flagship Brisbane store, the identity comes to life through immersive idents inspired by the Australian environment — turning retail into a felt experience of connectedness and energy, not a showroom of devices.

#### The Proof

### The strongest brand in Australia — built to behave, not just appear.

Optus was named the strongest brand in Australia by Brand Finance. The system was built to be highly practical while still expressive — supporting a complex, evolving organisation at scale.

### YOUI: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### How does an insurer behave with genuine empathy at scale — without tipping into staged sincerity?

In a category defined by fine print and abstract promises, Youi's strength was being "on people's level." But as they scaled, they needed a system that didn't just say they were customer-first — it had to act like it. The challenge: move beyond a static identity into a living brand system that could adapt, respond, and simplify the overwhelming world of insurance in real-time.

#### The Designed Behaviour

### We turned the logo into a functional device — one that actively puts the customer at the centre.

We turned the Youi logo into a functional behavioural device: The Lens. Instead of a static mark, the brand behaves as a spotlight — physically moving to put the customer at the centre of every interaction. The Lens holds typography, frames photography, and organises complex product and price information. It doesn't just sit on the page; it actively filters the world to make it easier for the customer to navigate.

#### Signature Behaviours

### A system that looks human, moves human, and sounds human.

- **The Lens as behaviour:** The logo is not decorative — it is a functional device that organises every layout around the customer's need.
- **The Human Motion Curve:** We moved away from the robotic, linear animations typical of financial services. A unique motion curve underpins every digital and broadcast interaction — fluid, dynamic, and slightly unpredictable, mimicking the cadence of human conversation.
- **Sonic Sync:** Working with Resonance, we matched the sonic logo to the motion curve. The brand doesn't just look human; it moves and sounds with a rhythmic, call-and-response behaviour that cuts through category noise.

#### Signature Moment: The Human Motion Curve

### The most invisible behaviour — and the most powerful.

Every digital and broadcast interaction is governed by a single, designed motion principle — non-linear, fluid, and human. It is the most invisible and most powerful behaviour in the system.

#### The Proof

### Customer-first, made functional — not just declared.

A deeply human brand world that feels candid rather than staged. The result is a system constantly adapting to the user's needs — proving that for Youi, "customer-first" isn't a slogan. It's a functional design principle.

### NEW ZEALAND RUGBY: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### This wasn't a logo problem. It was a behaviour problem.

New Zealand Rugby faced a critical inflection point. Women's rugby was experiencing astronomical growth — but the All Blacks and Black Ferns were operating under separate brand systems. The sport's future depended on one thing: making both teams behave with equal authority, equal investment, equal cultural weight.

#### The Designed Behaviour

### We designed equality to be felt — not just declared.

We didn't just refresh an identity. We designed a unified behavioural system that made equality visible and felt across every touchpoint — from broadcast graphics to on-field signage to digital experience.

#### Signature Behaviours

### Four design decisions that made structural change visible.

- **Parity in Motion:** A motion language that treats both teams identically in broadcast, digital and environmental contexts. No hierarchy. No "women's version." Same energy, same craft, same presence.
- **The Unified Crest System:** A single crest that behaves differently depending on context (All Blacks / Black Ferns) but maintains visual and cultural parity. The crest doesn't diminish; it adapts.
- **Parallel Storytelling:** A verbal and visual system that tells the story of both teams simultaneously — not sequentially. The brand behaves as an ecosystem, not a hierarchy.
- **Equal Broadcast Presence:** Both teams occupy the same visual real estate, with the same production value and the same motion language.

#### Signature Moment: The Unified Crest

### One crest. Two teams. Equal weight.

The design decision that made structural change visible before a single word was spoken.

#### The Proof

### A system that gave the organisation permission to invest equally.

The brand now behaves as a statement of intent: women's rugby is not a secondary story — it is the future of the sport. Internally, it gave the organisation permission to invest equally. Externally, it signalled to fans, sponsors and broadcasters that this wasn't tokenism — it was structural change. The Black Ferns' visibility and sponsorship value increased measurably.

### IMAGINE LEARNING: Brand that behaves

#### The Behavioural Challenge

### How do you unify a house of brands around a single behaviour — without losing what makes each product work?

How do you transform a massive portfolio of educational tools into a single, cohesive experience that encourages breakthrough learning — without flattening the distinctiveness of each product?

#### The Designed Behaviour

### We designed the brand to behave as a catalyst — celebrating the step, not just the result.

We designed the brand to behave as a "Catalyst for Breakthroughs." The system moves past the hype of EdTech and focuses on making real progress tangible — for students and teachers alike.

#### Signature Behaviours

### Three ways the brand makes progress visible and felt.

- **The Progress System:** The brand moves away from "assessment" and toward "momentum" — designing a visual language that celebrates the step as much as the result.
- **Unified Experience Architecture:** A complex house of brands consolidated into a single behavioural system. Whether a student is in a math module or a reading lab, the brand behaves with the same encouraging, breakthrough energy.
- **Outcome-Led UI:** The interface is designed around the moments where a student "gets it" — the breakthrough moment — not around product features or data dashboards.

#### The Proof

### A global education leader, refocused on what actually matters.

A global education leader refocused on its core mission: encouraging the next generation of breakthrough learning through a unified, progress-led experience.
